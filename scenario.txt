AARADHYA SETU - PUJA BOOKING SCENARIO
=====================================

This file explains, in simple English, how a booking starts on the customer side
and what the admin does with it once it arrives. The dashboard we are building
(Option 2) is the ADMIN side of this story.


PART 1 - CUSTOMER VIEW (the devotee)
------------------------------------

Meet Priya. She lives in Bengaluru and wants a Rudrabhishek puja done in Varanasi
for her father's health, but she cannot travel there.

1. Priya opens www.aaradhyasetu.com on her phone.

2. She browses the list of pujas (Rudrabhishek, Satyanarayan Katha,
   Navagraha Shanti, Lakshmi Puja, and so on). Each puja shows a photo,
   a short description, the temple, the benefits and the price.

3. She taps "Rudrabhishek" and reads the details.

4. She fills in a short form:
     - Her name (the devotee)
     - Name(s) of the people the puja is for, and their gotra
     - Her WhatsApp number and email
     - The date she wants the puja done
     - A sankalp / wish (for example: "good health for my father")
     - Any add-ons (extra offerings like flowers, prasad delivery)

5. She sees the total amount, for example Rs 2,100, and pays online.

6. The website shows: "Your booking is placed! Booking ID: AS-1024".
   She also gets the same message on WhatsApp / email.

7. Right now her booking is PENDING. That means:
   "We have your request and payment. Our team still has to confirm it."

8. Later, Priya receives updates as the booking moves forward:
     - CONFIRMED   -> "A pandit and a time slot are fixed for your puja."
     - IN PROGRESS -> "Your puja is happening now."
     - COMPLETED   -> "Your puja is done. Video / photos / prasad are on the way."

From Priya's side, that's it: book, pay, get updates.


PART 2 - ADMIN VIEW (the Aaradhya Setu team)
--------------------------------------------

Meet Rahul. He works on the operations team and handles bookings.

1. Rahul opens the Admin Dashboard (the app we are building).

2. He sees a table of all bookings. Each row shows:
     Booking ID | Devotee | Puja | Puja Date | Amount | Status

3. Priya's new booking, AS-1024, appears with status PENDING.

4. Rahul can find bookings quickly:
     - SEARCH  : type "Priya" or "AS-1024" or "Rudrabhishek"
     - FILTER  : show only Pending bookings, or only one puja type
     - SORT    : by puja date (what is coming up soon) or by amount

5. He clicks on AS-1024 to open the BOOKING DETAILS view. There he sees
   everything Priya entered: phone, email, people the puja is for, gotra,
   sankalp, temple, add-ons, amount paid, and when the booking was made.

6. Rahul calls the temple, fixes a pandit for that date, and then changes
   the status from PENDING to CONFIRMED.
   The change is saved in the database, so everyone on the team sees it.

7. On the puja day, when the pandit starts, Rahul changes it to IN PROGRESS.

8. When the puja is over and the video is ready, he changes it to COMPLETED.

9. At the top of the dashboard Rahul also sees simple counts, for example:
     Pending: 5 | Confirmed: 12 | In Progress: 2 | Completed: 40
   so he knows at a glance how much work is waiting.


THE STATUS LIFECYCLE (one line)
-------------------------------

   PENDING  ->  CONFIRMED  ->  IN PROGRESS  ->  COMPLETED
   (new)        (pandit &     (puja is        (puja done,
                 date fixed)   happening)      video/prasad sent)


WHAT IS IN SCOPE FOR THIS ASSIGNMENT
------------------------------------

In scope (we build this):
  - Admin dashboard: table, search, filters, sorting, details view
  - Changing status (Pending / Confirmed / In Progress / Completed)
  - Backend APIs + MongoDB so changes are saved
  - Works on mobile and desktop
  - Dummy bookings (seed data) so the dashboard has something to show

Out of scope (mocked or skipped):
  - The real customer website and online payment
    (we only pretend bookings came from it, using seed data)
  - Real WhatsApp / email messages
  - Admin login (can be added later if time allows)
